Understanding CTI scores
Why should I trust CTI?+

CTI uses publicly reported payment data and an independent methodology. Companies cannot influence their score through applications, references, explanations or supporting statements.

Why does this score matter?+

The CTI Score transforms complex payment reporting data into a structured indicator of payment behaviour, helping users identify trends, consistency and performance over time.

What does a CTI score mean?+

CTI helps users understand what reported payment behaviour may indicate about consistency, reliability and performance. It is designed to provide additional insight that can support procurement, due diligence and business decision-making.

What action should I take based on a score?+

CTI should be used as an additional source of information alongside broader due diligence and decision-making processes.

Is this company improving or deteriorating?+

Trend indicators compare reporting periods and highlight whether payment behaviour appears to be improving, stable or deteriorating over time.

How does this compare to similar businesses?+

Where available, CTI benchmarks performance against sector averages and peer groups to provide additional context.

What does a good CTI score look like?+

Higher scores generally indicate stronger payment performance, greater consistency and fewer negative behavioural indicators.

Why has a score changed?+

Scores may change due to:

  • New reporting periods becoming available
  • Changes in payment performance
  • Movement in behavioural indicators
  • Methodology enhancements
How recent is the data?+

CTI uses the most recent publicly reported payment data available and displays the reporting period used within the platform.

Can companies influence their score?+

No. Scores are generated using publicly reported data and CTI methodology. Companies cannot alter their score through applications, references or supporting evidence.

What are the limitations of the data?+

CTI relies on publicly reported information and can only assess what has been disclosed through payment reporting requirements. It should therefore be used alongside wider due diligence and business judgement.

General questions
What is CTI?+

Commercial Trust Index (CTI) is a behavioural intelligence platform that transforms publicly reported payment data into structured insight, helping organisations better understand payment performance and make more informed decisions.

Is CTI a credit score?+

No. CTI is not a credit score, credit rating or financial solvency assessment.

Does CTI predict future payment behaviour?+

No. CTI provides insight into historical payment behaviour and trends based on reported data.

Where does the data come from?+

CTI uses publicly reported UK payment practices and performance data.

Who is CTI for?+

CTI is designed for:

  • Suppliers & contractors
  • Developers
  • Procurement teams
  • Consultants
  • Lenders & insurers
  • Industry stakeholders
How is CTI different from PaymentCheck?+

PaymentCheck primarily presents payment reporting information and scoring. CTI focuses on behavioural insight, trend analysis, benchmarking and interpretation, helping users understand what payment behaviour may indicate over time.

How is CTI different from the Fair Payment Code?+

The Fair Payment Code assesses commitment to payment standards and principles. CTI assesses observable payment behaviour using publicly reported data. The two approaches are complementary and provide different perspectives.

Will CTI expand beyond construction?+

Yes. Construction is the initial launch sector, but the longer-term vision is to create a scalable Commercial Trust Index that can be applied across multiple industries.

About Us

Who we are and why CTI exists.

Commercial Trust Index (CTI) was created to bridge the gap between publicly available payment data and practical commercial insight. Rather than simply presenting raw reporting information, CTI transforms payment reporting data into a structured commercial behaviour assessment that helps organisations understand what the data actually means.

The problem we saw

A significant visibility gap

Every day, organisations make critical commercial decisions about who they work with, who they appoint, and who they trust within their supply chains. Yet despite increasing levels of transparency, many of those decisions are still made with limited visibility of how companies actually behave commercially.

Payment reporting requirements have made large volumes of information publicly available — but access to data does not automatically create understanding. Much of this information remains difficult to interpret, compare, and apply in real-world decision-making.

Organisations often lack visibility of

  • How consistently a company pays its suppliers
  • Whether payment behaviour is improving or deteriorating
  • Commercial trends indicating changing behaviour
  • How one organisation compares with others
  • Potential payment concerns before entering relationships

Greater transparency benefits

  • Procurement teams — stronger supplier assessment tools
  • Suppliers — greater confidence in commercial partners
  • Investors — better visibility into commercial behaviour
  • Industry bodies — clearer insight into market performance
  • Businesses — greater confidence in new relationships

CTI was built to bridge this gap — transforming complex payment data into clear, actionable commercial insight that supports better decision-making.

Our response

Using a consistent methodology, CTI converts publicly reported payment information into commercial behaviour scores, trend analysis, behavioural indicators, market comparisons, and structured commercial insight. The objective is not to replace professional judgement, accreditation schemes, or financial assessments — our purpose is to provide an additional layer of visibility that supports more informed commercial decision-making.

Our mission

To improve visibility of commercial payment behaviour and help organisations make more informed decisions.

We believe that when payment behaviour becomes easier to understand, businesses can make better choices, commercial relationships become stronger, and markets become more transparent and accountable.

Our long-term vision

A CTI Score for every business

Today, CTI is built using publicly reported payment information, providing a structured and consistent view of commercial payment behaviour among larger organisations. But this represents only part of the wider business community.

Our long-term ambition is to create a future where every business has a CTI Score, regardless of its size, sector, turnover, or number of employees. We believe transparency should not be limited to organisations that meet statutory reporting thresholds.

Building a more transparent commercial environment

CTI is built on a simple principle: you cannot improve what you cannot see. By transforming complex payment reporting data into meaningful insight, we aim to support a more transparent, accountable, and commercially informed business environment for businesses of every size — from sole traders and small enterprises to multinational organisations.

Methodology & Assessment Overview

How the CTI score is built and what it measures.

The Commercial Trust Index (CTI) is an independent, data-driven measure of commercial payment behaviour within the UK business sector. Rather than focusing on policies, commitments, memberships or certifications, CTI assesses observable behaviour using objective data published under statutory reporting requirements.

Data sources

What we use

  • UK Government Payment Practices Reporting data (current model)
  • Statutory corporate filings (future models)
  • Other relevant public records where applicable (future models)

What we don’t use

  • Surveys or self-assessments
  • Confidential information
  • Applications, references or supporting statements from rated companies
What CTI measures

CTI evaluates indicators grouped into four core assessment areas, providing a broader picture than any single payment statistic alone.

Payment Behaviour

Measures how promptly organisations pay suppliers and how consistently payments are made within recognised payment periods.

Reporting & Governance Signals

Assesses the quality, consistency and completeness of publicly reported information.

Payment Friction Indicators

Considers signals that may indicate payment delays, disputes or payments occurring outside agreed terms.

Trend & Stability Analysis

Evaluates whether payment performance is improving, deteriorating or remaining consistent over time.

Higher scores indicate

  • Stronger payment performance
  • More consistent payment behaviour
  • Greater stability over time
  • Fewer indicators of payment friction

Lower scores may indicate

  • Slower payment practices
  • Greater variability in behaviour
  • Deteriorating trends
  • Increased payment friction signals

A lower score does not imply financial distress, insolvency risk or future business performance.

A behavioural index, not a snapshot

CTI is designed as a rolling index. Rather than relying on a single reporting period, the methodology considers recent reporting history to provide a more balanced and representative assessment of behaviour. This approach helps reduce the impact of short-term fluctuations and rewards sustained performance over time.

Core principles

Comparability

All organisations are assessed using the same methodology and data standards. CTI does not accept payment in exchange for altering, improving, suppressing or removing scores.

How CTI can be used

CTI helps organisations

  • Compare payment behaviour across companies
  • Identify differences in supplier payment performance
  • Support procurement and supply chain assessments
  • Monitor behavioural trends over time
  • Improve transparency in commercial decision-making

Methodology governance

CTI methodologies are version controlled and subject to periodic review. Changes are documented and published through formal version updates to maintain consistency and comparability over time.

To preserve the integrity of the index and prevent manipulation, detailed scoring calculations, weighting structure, calibration process and risk triggers remain proprietary.

Important information

CTI is not a credit rating, financial assessment, certification, accreditation or recommendation. It is an independent, data-driven measure of observed commercial payment behaviour, designed to support transparency and informed decision-making. Users should consider CTI alongside other relevant information when making commercial, procurement or business decisions.

CTI measures observed commercial behaviour using publicly available data. It does not assess opinions, memberships, certifications or future outcomes.
